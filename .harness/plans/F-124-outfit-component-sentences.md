## Intent

`scoreOutfit` emits a `messageKey` per component and **none of the eighteen is in either
catalogue.** `OutfitBuilder` renders `${c.component}: ${score}` — the raw identifier, in English,
in both locales. A Japanese reader sees six English words beside six numbers.

Done: every key in `OUTFIT_MESSAGE_KEYS` has a written sentence in `en` and `ja`, the builder
renders the sentence, and `i18n.test.ts`'s declared gap is **deleted** rather than shortened.

## Approach

**Eighteen keys, six components × three directions**, generated by the engine as
`outfit.<component>.<direction>`. The sentences follow the register `explain.*` established in
F-030: one short second-person sentence, saying what was measured rather than asserting a
verdict.

Two are worth stating because they are the ones easiest to get wrong:

- **`corpusAffinity` is not "how Japanese this is."** The engine's own header and ADR-0073 say
  so. The copy says *close to colours in the collection*, which is what ΔE00 to the nearest
  reference entry actually measures.
- **`cvdAccessibility` matches `cvd.none`'s existing wording** — *"the three common colour-vision
  types"* — rather than inventing a second way to describe the same simulation.

**`isMessageKey` moves to `src/i18n/index.ts`.** It exists in `Shopping.tsx` and `OutfitBuilder`
now needs it; a second copy of a narrowing helper is a second answer to one question. Both
screens import it.

## The test change is the delicate half

`i18n.test.ts`'s unused-key scan is a **source-literal scan**, and these keys are rendered
through `t(c.messageKey)` — a computed lookup with no literal to find. So they must join the
`dynamic` exclusion set, and **that exclusion is only safe because the set is pinned in both
directions**, exactly as F-052 arranged for `explain.*`:

1. the engine emits no key the catalogue lacks, **and**
2. the catalogue declares no `outfit.<component>.<direction>` the engine does not emit.

Without the second, an outfit key nobody emits could sit in the catalogue forever — the
placeholder shape the scan exists to catch.

**The reverse pin must not swallow the existing `outfit.*` keys.** `outfit.title`, `outfit.overall`
and the rest have **two** dot-segments; the engine's have **three**. The filter is on segment
count, and a test asserts the partition is non-empty on both sides — otherwise a filter matching
nothing would pass.

## Test plan

- **The gap test is deleted, not shortened.** Its replacement is the positive assertion that
  every `OUTFIT_MESSAGE_KEYS` entry is in `en` — the same shape the `explain.*` assertion has.
- **The reverse pin**, and a check that both sides of the segment-count partition are non-empty.
- **The sentence actually reaches the screen.** `screens.test.tsx` renders the builder and
  asserts a component sentence is in the text and the raw identifier is **not** — because a
  registry subject that silently kept rendering `harmony: 78` would pass conformance perfectly.
- **Mutation:** render the raw component name again, and drop the `ja` entries, and confirm the
  suite goes red in each case.
- **Not applicable:** `color-golden`, `cvd`, `perf` — no engine change. `e2e` — gate 7, F-091.

## Risks and open questions

- **No `OQ-*`.**
- **Eighteen Japanese sentences are written by a non-native speaker**, like the rest of `ja.ts`.
  `JA_REVIEWED` is empty and OQ-5 is open; the count of unreviewed entries the suite reports
  goes up by eighteen, which is the honest record rather than a problem this feature can fix.
- **The screen gets longer.** Six sentences where six short labels were. That is the requirement
  — FR-32 asks for component scores that mean something — but it is a real change to the shape
  of the panel and the contrast gate will be checking six more lines of body text.

## Out of scope

- **Rendering the `evidence` numbers.** `ComponentScore` carries what each component looked at,
  and showing it is a second design question about how much a builder screen should explain.
  Not filed: no criterion asks, and FR-32's own words are satisfied by the score and its
  sentence.
- **The `factors` decomposition.** Shopping renders it for `scoreColor`; the outfit builder does
  not, and adding it here would be scope nobody reviewed.
